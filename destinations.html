Paro Valley & The Sacred Cliff of Taktsang

Paro is a broad, fertile valley dotted with willow trees, traditional wooden farmsteads, and the serpentine Pa Chhu river. Perched 900 meters above the valley floor on a sheer granite cliff hangs Paro Taktsang (Tiger’s Nest), where Guru Rinpoche flew on the back of a tigress in the 8th century.

Best Season: Oct–May (Clear Skies)
Key Dzong: Rinpung & Ta Dzong
Highlight: Tiger’s Nest Hike (4–5 hrs)
Stay Type: 5★ Luxury & Heritage Farm

The Seat of Government & Faith

Thimphu: The World’s Only Capital Without Traffic Lights

Thimphu blends ancient monastic traditions with modern Bhutanese creativity. Watch white-gloved traffic officers direct cars with balletic grace, marvel at the colossal 51.5-meter golden Buddha Dordenma, explore royal weaving schools, and watch monks debate at Tashichho Dzong.

Altitude: 2,334m / 7,657ft
Drive from Paro: 1 hr 15 mins (smooth highway)
Highlight: 169ft Golden Buddha Statue
Culture: School of 13 Traditional Arts

Punakha: Jacaranda Blossoms & Fortress of Bliss

At an elevation of just 1,242 meters, Punakha is warm, lush, and filled with orange orchards and terraced rice paddies. Punakha Dzong, built in 1637, sits majestically at the confluence of the Pho Chhu (Male) and Mo Chhu (Female) rivers and is widely celebrated as the most breathtaking dzong in Bhutan.

Climate: Warm & Sub-tropical (T-shirt weather)
Via: Dochula Pass (108 Stupas)
Bridge: 160m Long Suspension Bridge
Adventures: Gentle White Water Rafting

Valley of the Black-Necked Cranes

Phobjikha & Gangtey: Vast Glacial Alpine Peace

A broad, U-shaped glacial valley of quiet beauty without overhead powerlines (buried underground to protect the birds). Every winter, hundreds of sacred Black-Necked Cranes fly over the Himalayas from Tibet to roost here, circling Gangtey Monastery three times upon arrival and departure.

Altitude: 3,000m / 9,840ft
Crane Season: Late Oct – Early March
Hike: Gangtey Nature Trail (1.5 hrs)
Monastery: 17th-century Gangtey Goemba
